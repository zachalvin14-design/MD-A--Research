# Project 1 — Edition A

## Decision and User

The decision is whether the fund should initiate a position in Costco Wholesale Corporation. The user is the fund's investment committee, which needs a clear recommendation supported by evidence, valuation discipline, and explicit risks.

## Company and Valuation Date

- **Company:** Costco Wholesale Corporation (NASDAQ: COST; CIK 0000909832)
- **Valuation date:** September 1, 2026

## Initial Thesis

**Watch-defer.** Costco appears to be a high-quality business with durable membership economics, strong customer retention, and a net cash position. However, business quality alone does not establish an attractive investment. I would defer initiation until the analysis incorporates the latest available financial results, a valuation based on the market price as of the valuation date, and a reasonable expected-return range.

## Known Evidence With Sources

- Costco reported fiscal 2025 net sales of **$269.912 billion**, membership-fee revenue of **$5.323 billion**, and operating income of **$10.383 billion**. Membership-fee revenue increased 10% from fiscal 2024.
- Fiscal 2025 comparable sales increased **6%**, supported by a 5% increase in shopping frequency and approximately 1% growth in average ticket.
- Year-end renewal rates were **92.3% in the U.S. and Canada** and **89.8% worldwide**, evidence of strong member retention.
- At August 31, 2025, Costco had **$14.161 billion of cash and cash equivalents**, **$1.123 billion of short-term investments**, and approximately **$5.788 billion of debt at net carrying value**. This is a net cash position before considering lease liabilities.
- Fiscal 2025 weighted-average diluted shares were **444.803 million**. The calculation included 0.818 million incremental shares from restricted stock units using the treasury-stock method.
- Costco operated **914 warehouses** at August 31, 2025, compared with 890 one year earlier.

Sources:

- [Costco fiscal 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983225000101/cost-20250831.htm)
- [SEC filing index: accession 0000909832-25-000101](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983225000101/0000909832-25-000101-index.htm)

## Consequential Assumptions

- Membership renewal rates remain near historical levels and support recurring fee revenue.
- New warehouses can generate attractive returns without materially weakening existing locations.
- Costco can preserve its low-price value proposition while managing wage, merchandise, and logistics costs.
- Membership fees remain a durable source of operating profit, but their growth should not be assumed to continue at 10% indefinitely.
- Cash and short-term investments are treated as non-operating assets in enterprise value; debt is included at net carrying value. Lease treatment will be made consistent with the valuation multiple used.
- A premium valuation is justified only if Costco can continue compounding revenue and cash flow with lower risk than the typical retailer.

## Unknowns

- Costco's share price and resulting equity and enterprise values on the valuation date.
- The latest operating results and material developments after the fiscal 2025 10-K.
- The valuation premium relative to Costco's own history and comparable companies.
- A defensible range for long-term comparable-sales growth, warehouse growth, margins, reinvestment needs, and free cash flow.
- The fund's required return, position-size limits, investment horizon, and benchmark constraints.
- The principal downside scenario and the potential permanent loss of capital if growth slows while the valuation multiple contracts.

## Partner-Generated Falsification Question

> What evidence would show that Costco's membership-fee growth and high renewal rates can no longer justify its valuation premium?

This question is intentionally unanswered in Edition A.

## Research Plan

1. Update the evidence through Costco's latest SEC filing and earnings release, reconciling all major figures to primary sources.
2. Record the valuation-date share price and calculate diluted equity value and enterprise value, showing the treatment of cash, short-term investments, debt, and leases.
3. Analyze five years of comparable sales, membership-fee revenue, renewal rates, warehouse count, margins, capital expenditures, and free cash flow.
4. Identify Costco's competitive advantages and test their durability against pricing, labor, sourcing, e-commerce, and competitive risks.
5. Value Costco with a discounted cash flow model and relevant trading multiples using consistent definitions.
6. Build bear, base, and bull cases with explicit assumptions, target values, expected returns, and probabilities.
7. Answer the falsification question with evidence and identify observable developments that would invalidate the thesis.
8. Recommend initiate, continue watching, or do not initiate based on expected return, downside risk, and fit with the fund's mandate.
