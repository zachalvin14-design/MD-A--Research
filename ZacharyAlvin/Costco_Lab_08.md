What would one share of Costco Wholesale Corporation be worth at defensible peer P/E multiples, and how does that valuation compare with my Week 3 discounted cash flow valuation?

## Target Company

Company: Costco Wholesale Corporation (NASDAQ: COST)

Costco operates membership-based warehouse clubs that sell a limited selection of merchandise at low prices and high sales volumes. Costco earns revenue primarily from merchandise sales and membership fees. A useful peer should therefore have meaningful similarities in large-scale retail, recurring customer traffic, low-price/high-volume merchandising, membership economics, or warehouse-club operations.

## Peer Selection Policy

I will consider publicly traded operating companies that have meaningful similarities to Costco's retail economics. Strong candidates should have large-scale consumer retail operations, significant grocery/general merchandise exposure, high inventory turnover, price-focused merchandising, and/or a membership-based retail model.

I will qualify companies that share important retail economics with Costco but have meaningful differences in business model, geographic exposure, membership structure, product mix, or scale.

I will exclude companies whose primary business economics differ substantially from Costco, even if they are commonly classified as retailers.

For valuation, I will use annual reported diluted EPS that was publicly available by my Week 3 valuation date and stock prices from the same trading date for Costco and each peer.

These preliminary classifications are based on operating comparability, not whether either company's valuation is convenient for the analysis.

## Peer P/E Calculations

The price-to-earnings ratio is calculated consistently as:

\[
\text{P/E} = \frac{\text{September 1, 2026 closing price}}{\text{annual reported diluted EPS}}
\]

| Peer | Closing price | Annual reported diluted EPS | Calculation | P/E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BJ's Wholesale Club | $91.48 | $4.38 | $91.48 ÷ $4.38 | **20.885845×** |
| Walmart | $105.92 | $2.73 | $105.92 ÷ $2.73 | **38.798535×** |

The two-peer range is **20.885845× to 38.798535×**. With two observations, the median is the average of the two multiples:

\[
\text{Median peer P/E} = \frac{20.885845 + 38.798535}{2} = 29.842190\times
\]

For context, Costco's own P/E on the same basis was **51.617792×** ($939.96 ÷ $18.21). Costco's own multiple is not included in the peer range or peer median because a target company should not be its own comparable company.

Unrounded P/E values are retained for the implied-share-price calculations; displayed values are rounded to six decimal places.

## Costco Implied Share Price from Peer P/E

Each peer multiple is converted into an estimated Costco share price using Costco's fiscal 2025 reported diluted EPS of $18.21:

\[
\text{Costco implied share price} = \text{Peer P/E} \times \$18.21
\]

| Valuation basis | P/E applied to Costco | Costco EPS | Implied Costco share price |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BJ's multiple | 20.885845× | $18.21 | **$380.33** |
| Median peer multiple | 29.842190× | $18.21 | **$543.43** |
| Walmart multiple | 38.798535× | $18.21 | **$706.52** |

The detailed calculations using unrounded peer multiples are:

\[
\frac{\$91.48}{\$4.38} \times \$18.21 = \$380.33
\]

\[
\frac{20.8858447489 + 38.7985347985}{2} \times \$18.21 = \$543.43
\]

\[
\frac{\$105.92}{\$2.73} \times \$18.21 = \$706.52
\]

Interpretation: if investors valued Costco's $18.21 of annual diluted earnings at BJ's P/E, Costco would be worth approximately **$380 per share**. At Walmart's qualified peer multiple, it would be worth approximately **$707 per share**. The median of the two peer multiples implies approximately **$543 per share**. Thus, the peer-based valuation range is **$380.33 to $706.52 per Costco share**.

## What Peer P/E Adds to the DCF

The DCF estimates Costco's intrinsic value from expected future cash flows, while the peer P/E approach provides a market-based reference for how investors valued reported earnings from comparable businesses on the valuation date. The peer valuation therefore acts as a useful cross-check on the assumptions embedded in the DCF. However, Costco's membership model, scale, growth expectations, and operating characteristics differ from its peers, so I would not expect the valuations to match exactly.

The fact that the entire peer range is generally above the DCF base case suggests that public markets assign stronger valuation multiples to comparable retail earnings than the DCF assumptions imply. It does not prove the DCF is wrong or that the peer values are intrinsic values. The overlap between the upper DCF sensitivity values and the BJ's-based peer estimate also shows that some of the difference can be explained by reasonable changes in DCF assumptions.

## Skeptical Colleague Review

The weakest-supported assumption is the DCF's **10% WACC**. It is explicitly labeled an unresolved coursework placeholder rather than a company-specific estimate built from a risk-free rate, beta, equity-risk premium, borrowing cost, tax rate, and market-value capital structure. Because terminal value represents a large portion of enterprise value, the chosen WACC materially affects the result.

The following alignment issues require attention:

- **Company:** No mismatch. Both methods value Costco.
- **Date:** There are two meaningful timing limitations. First, the DCF uses Costco fiscal 2025 cash flow and balance-sheet inputs because complete fiscal 2026 results were not public on September 1, 2026. Second, the saved DCF script's reverse-DCF and market-price comparison use a $902.60 closing price from September 9, 2026. The $320.12 intrinsic value and $260.62–$426.65 sensitivity range do not depend on that market-price input, so they remain usable here, but the saved September 9 reverse-DCF conclusion is not treated as a September 1 result. The P/E analysis uses September 1 prices consistently and uses Costco fiscal 2025 EPS versus BJ's and Walmart fiscal years ending January 31, 2026.
- **Valuation object:** No material mismatch. The DCF produces equity value per diluted share after adding cash and short-term investments and subtracting debt. The P/E method directly estimates equity value per share from diluted EPS.
- **Earnings definition:** The P/E analysis consistently uses annual reported GAAP diluted EPS, not adjusted EPS or a single quarter. However, the fiscal periods are not perfectly synchronized, and P/E focuses on accounting earnings while the DCF focuses on FCFF.
- **Peer comparability:** BJ's is directly comparable in business model but much smaller and less geographically diversified. Walmart offers scale, grocery exposure, purchasing power, and Sam's Club, but the consolidated company includes extensive traditional retail, e-commerce, international, and other operations. Two peers provide a thin evidence base.

**One decision-changing question:** What company-specific WACC is supported by market evidence as of September 1, 2026, and would the DCF still remain below Costco's market price across a defensible range around that WACC?

## Conclusion

I used BJ's Wholesale Club as a direct peer because its membership-based warehouse model most closely resembles Costco's business economics. I qualified Walmart as a broader comparison because it shares Costco's large-scale, low-price grocery and general merchandise economics but does not rely primarily on the membership warehouse model.

The peer P/E analysis implies a Costco value of approximately **$380.33 to $706.52 per share**, compared with my Week 3 DCF valuation of **$260.62 to $426.65 per share**.

The difference between the two approaches is primarily driven by the DCF's forecast cash-flow growth and discount-rate assumptions—especially its unsourced 10% placeholder WACC—versus the market multiples embedded in BJ's and Walmart's September 1, 2026 share prices. Peer selection and the companies' different business models and fiscal periods also affect the P/E range. I do not mechanically average the two estimates because the DCF and peer P/E approaches answer different valuation questions and rely on different assumptions.

My current call remains **watch-defer** because Costco's September 1, 2026 closing price of $939.96 exceeds both valuation ranges. I would reconsider this conclusion if Costco's complete fiscal 2026 results and a sourced company-specific WACC support a defensible intrinsic value near the market price, or if the share price falls into a supported valuation range while the company's membership economics, cash-flow growth, and competitive advantages remain intact.
