# Meridian Foundation — assigned common case

Scope: the $100 million reserve, S1–S10, and the supplied holdings and candidates only.

## 1. What is an investment policy statement?

An **investment policy statement (IPS)** is a written agreement that guides how a portfolio is managed. It records the investor's goals, risk tolerance, investment restrictions, spending needs, and decision-making responsibilities. For Meridian, it should tell the CIO what the reserve is supposed to achieve, which investments and actions are permitted, and how results and breaches will be evaluated.

## 2. How does it work: objectives and constraints?

**Objectives describe the desired outcome.** Meridian's stated return objective is CPI + 4% per year, net of costs, measured over rolling five-year periods. This is a target to pursue and evaluate, not a guaranteed result. The CIO must also define an operational risk objective and decide what the pilot should optimize.

**Constraints define the boundaries within which the portfolio must operate.** Meridian's boundaries include payout funding, liquidity, permitted instruments, issuer concentration, turnover, and the investment horizon. A portfolio can pass these boundaries and still be unsuitable for the objective: 100% cash passes the coded checks but does not establish a credible path to S2.

In practice, the manager first clarifies the IPS, screens portfolios against its constraints, then evaluates feasible portfolios against its objectives. A benchmark provides a comparison for performance; it becomes an allocation restriction only if the IPS explicitly makes it one. Monitoring and approval rules explain when to rebalance, who may authorize exceptions, and what happens after a breach.

## What the CIO must answer before optimization

- **Does S2’s CPI + 4% net return objective apply to the entire portfolio, including the liquidity reserve, and over what investment horizon?**
- **What should the pilot optimize?** Specify the criterion and trade-offs—for example, probability of achieving S2, expected net real return, or risk relative to the benchmark—and how payouts enter the analysis.
- **What does S9 mean operationally?** Define loss measurement (including treatment of payouts and costs), the risk metric and acceptable probability or scenario standard, and the action required after a breach; clarify whether the committee intends an absolute guarantee or a measurable risk limit.
- **How are the constraints measured?** Confirm issuer aggregation, fund look-through and sovereign exceptions for S6; the turnover convention and approval process for S7; and whether S8 means redemption requests accepted or cash available within five business days, including stressed conditions.
- **How do the instructions interact?** Confirm how quarterly payouts and cash replenishment fit annual rebalancing, whether S4 carries any binding allocation or tracking limits, and which CPI series and annualized return convention govern S2.
